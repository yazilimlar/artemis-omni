# Heritage Mode

## Definition

Heritage Mode creates historically informed botanical visual treatments. It is not a
claim that the plant was depicted in that period unless a source says so. It is a visual
mode for educational and museum-style presentation.

## Approved Heritage Styles

| Style | Background | Ink / Detail | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Renaissance study | antique paper | iron gall | anatomical sketches and measured notes |
| Victorian botanical | cream watercolor paper | muted green | pressed-flower and atlas tradition |
| Japanese botanical | rice paper | indigo | minimal composition and careful negative space |
| Classical archive | limestone or papyrus | olive or bronze | use only as a visual frame unless sourced |
| Copperplate engraving | warm paper | brown-black line | useful for morphology and labels |

## Required Labels

Every Heritage Mode asset must include:

- visual mode,
- generated or source-based status,
- source photo reference,
- specimen ID,
- claim status for historical text,
- version.

## Prohibited Behavior

- Do not imply an ancient culture used or named a plant without documentation.
- Do not add fake marginalia that looks like a real historical source.
- Do not turn traditional medicinal or culinary notes into recommendations.
- Do not obscure the distinction between source photography and generated art.

## Hemerocallis fulva Direction

The first reference mode should use Victorian botanical plate language because it is
closest to museum herbarium presentation and avoids unsupported ancient claims.
