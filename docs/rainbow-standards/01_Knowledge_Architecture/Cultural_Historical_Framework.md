# Cultural and Historical Framework

## Purpose

Rainbow Botanics should preserve cultural, linguistic, historical, artistic, and
symbolic context without inventing unsupported stories. This framework defines how to
handle those layers credibly.

## Claim Classes

| Class | Meaning | Publication Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified | Supported by current botanical, institutional, or peer-reviewed references. | Publish with citation. |
| Historical | Supported by dated historical texts, herbals, catalogs, garden records, or archives. | Publish with source and date when possible. |
| Traditional | Attributed to community practice, folklore, garden tradition, or regional use. | Publish only with attribution and no universal framing. |
| Interpretive | Rainbow Botanics engineering, visual, or curatorial analysis. | Publish as interpretation, not external fact. |
| Needs source | Draft idea awaiting evidence. | Do not publish as fact. |

## Required Cultural Subsections

- Common and regional names
- Historical names and synonyms
- Etymology
- Garden and cultivation history
- Literature and botanical writing
- Art and illustration references
- Symbolism and flower-language claims where documented
- Traditional culinary or medicinal mentions, clearly labeled as historical or
  traditional and not as current advice

## Mythology Rules

- If a species has no documented mythological association, state that no documented
  mythology has been confirmed yet.
- Do not create Greek, Roman, Persian, Ottoman, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, or other
  associations without sources.
- Later symbolism must be separated from ancient or classical references.

## Example: Hemerocallis fulva

Acceptable draft items:

- Common English names: Orange Daylily, Tawny Daylily, Ditch Lily, Roadside Lily.
- Historical or regional name candidate: Wash-house Lily, marked `needs_source` unless
  a reliable regional reference is attached.
- Etymology: *Hemerocallis* is commonly understood from Greek roots for day and beauty;
  cite a botanical etymology source before public release.
- Cultural layer: East Asian origin and later spread through Europe and North America,
  with introduction timeline marked for citation.

## Review Checklist

- Does every claim have a source status?
- Are traditions attributed to a place, period, or source?
- Are medicinal or edible notes framed as historical/traditional only?
- Are uncertain names and stories held in draft state?
- Are AI-generated summaries checked against sources before public publishing?
