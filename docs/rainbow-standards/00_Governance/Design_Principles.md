# Design Principles

## 1. Specimen First

Every page, poster, map, and social asset starts from a specimen record. A species page
can aggregate multiple specimens, but the durable unit is the observed plant or colony.

## 2. Digital Twin, Not Static Post

A record should accumulate observations over time. New yearly photos, bloom dates,
health notes, pollinator sightings, and weather context enrich the same asset instead of
creating disconnected posts.

## 3. Layers, Not Long Walls of Text

Knowledge should be organized into switchable layers: scientific, linguistic,
geographic, historical, cultural, mythological, literary, artistic, engineering,
ecological, AI, and Atlas.

## 4. Ground Truth Photography

User-captured photos are the record anchor. AI-generated posters, sketches, diagrams,
and renderings are derivative assets and must remain tied to the source photo set.

## 5. Adaptive Presentation

The interface should adapt to the organism. Colors, diagrams, maps, and visual modes
should inherit from species palette, habitat, season, geography, and knowledge context.

## 6. Source Transparency

The system must distinguish:

- Verified: supported by reliable botanical or geographic sources.
- Historical: documented in historical sources.
- Traditional: attributed to cultural practice, folklore, or regional use.
- Interpretive: analytical framing created by Rainbow Botanics.
- Needs source: useful draft content that cannot be published as fact yet.

## 7. Public-safe by Default

Exact private GPS coordinates, raw EXIF location trails, personal property details, and
private storage URLs are never public by default. Public pages use rounded locations,
aliases, or owner-approved Atlas views.

## 8. Human Approval

AI agents draft, compare, and critique. Humans approve final facts, visuals, publishing,
account access, and production deployment.
