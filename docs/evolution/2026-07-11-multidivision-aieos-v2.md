## What Changed

Artemis was formally defined as an AI-native multi-division umbrella organization and product platform.

Infrastructure, construction intelligence, CivicBid, utility intelligence, project controls, 5D cost, forecasting, cashflow, claims, and risk remain a flagship public focus. They no longer define the full boundary of Artemis.

The accepted architecture also provides room for:

- Artemis Core Platform
- Atlas & Places
- Studio & Media
- Knowledge & Academy
- Finance & Decision Systems
- Natural Systems
- Artemis Labs as a cross-division incubator

These divisions are architectural and product domains. They are not automatically claims of legally separate entities, staffed departments, or production-ready products.

## Governance Added

The AIEOS extension added authoritative internal registries for:

- divisions,
- products and maturity,
- testbed capability migration,
- visibility and data modes,
- canonical implementations and donor branches.

ArtemisIX19 is classified as a testbed and cross-division capability donor. It must not be discarded before its verified capabilities receive evidence-based dispositions.

## Public Memorialization Added

A clean public record was designed separately from the internal engineering registries:

- `/evolution` — human-readable public structure and evolution page
- `/api/public/artemis-structure` — machine-readable public JSON
- `data/artemisPublicStructure.ts` — shared source model
- `docs/evolution/PUBLIC_EVOLUTION_POLICY.md` — publication and revision rules

The public record labels statements as current, directional, or historical and explains the distinction between Artemis divisions and the client departments Artemis serves.

## Why the Records Are Separate

The internal record must be precise enough to govern branches, migrations, security, and production. Publishing all of that detail would create noise and could expose sensitive operations.

The public record therefore publishes verified structure, maturity labels, review principles, and milestones while withholding credentials, private data, security-sensitive configuration, confidential strategy, and unnecessary donor-branch mechanics.

## Control and Traceability

Major changes should leave a chain of evidence:

1. accepted decision,
2. updated registries,
3. isolated branch,
4. exact pull-request diff,
5. automated validation,
6. deployed preview,
7. human approval,
8. merge to the stable integration line,
9. dated evolution note,
10. public record update when appropriate.

## Anti-Roadblock Rule

The architecture is durable but revisable. It protects evidence, public safety, and continuity without freezing temporary names or preventing future divisions and products from being reorganized through a later accepted decision.
