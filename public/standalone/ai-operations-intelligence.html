AttemptBook™

AI Unit-Economics Ledger Specification

AI work is billed by providers per attempt — every call, retry, and fallback — but it is accounted by businesses per project, and the two never reconcile. AttemptBook is the standard that closes that gap: an attempt-level, evidence-classified, append-only ledger in which every billed attempt is accounted, every cost carries its provenance, and the books reconcile to the provider invoice.

Standard adopted · Nov 2026 Public specification in drafting Software R0.1 in design & testing No live data on this page

The standard for ArtLoop, ArtPlex & OctopusG — by ARTEMIS Construction Intelligence

Why it existsThree failures of ordinary AI cost tracking

Blended numbers

Most dashboards merge actual, estimated, and guessed costs into one figure. A total you cannot decompose is a total you cannot audit. AttemptBook keeps every amount classified by its evidence — actual, observed, declared, estimated, simulated, or unknown.

Invisible retries

Failed calls and retries are real charges that vanish from per-request accounting. In AttemptBook the attempt is the atomic unit: a run that took three attempts costs three attempts, and retry overhead is reported, not hidden.

Rewritten history

When provider prices change, naive systems silently recalculate the past. AttemptBook pins every posting to the price-book version in force when the cost was incurred, and corrects errors only by compensating entries — never by editing history.

Principles

  • Unknown is not zero. A total with unknown charges is reported as incomplete — never silently rounded down.
  • Provenance never promotes itself. A declared figure stays declared until evidence supports more, and the promotion is itself a recorded event.
  • Allocation conserves. Shared costs are distributed by explicit, recorded drivers; whatever cannot be attributed stays visibly unallocated.
  • Invoices prove totals. Reconciliation verifies the account total; per-attempt attribution stands on its own evidence.
  • Tamper-evident, honestly. The audit chain detects alteration against independently kept checkpoints. We claim exactly that — no more.

Evidence classes

  • actual — verified against an invoice or financial record, at the scope the evidence covers.
  • observed — reported by a provider or directly metered; not yet invoice-reconciled.
  • declared — supplied by a named person, on the record.
  • estimated — computed from stated assumptions or allocated by estimated drivers.
  • simulated — hypothetical; never mixed into operating totals.
  • unknown — an explicit gap in the record, shown as a gap.

Engineering notesHow it is being built

Architecture

  • Two surfaces, by design. A public educational surface — this page — carries no database, no operating data, and no financial figures. The internal application is separately access-controlled and fails closed.
  • Ledger before interface. The first release is a reconciliation system, not a dashboard: schema, calculation, and tests precede any interface work.
  • Decimal money only. All amounts are computed in fixed-precision decimal arithmetic and stored with their original currency, rate, and rounding basis.
  • Idempotent by construction. The same event submitted twice posts once; the same key with different content is rejected and quarantined, never overwritten.

Testing

  • A public conformance test suite is being drafted with the specification: synthetic fixtures, hand-checkable arithmetic, and pass/fail conditions covering retries, cache pricing, price-book versioning, allocation conservation, reconciliation, and access control.
  • Every figure used in testing is fabricated and labelled synthetic. No client, project, or operating data is used in any test or on any public page.
  • Development follows a gated sequence — specification, repository verification, vertical slice, protected release — with independent review at each gate.
