HVAC Mechanical Standards Lens

Purpose: show how SMACNA, ASHRAE, ARI, and NYC Mechanical Code standard families feed a mechanical model that becomes quantity takeoff, ERP cost codes, field production, schedule, cashflow, variance, risk, opportunity, and executive reporting.

Duct families
Galv Rect · Spiral · Flex
Rectangular and spiral duct selections drive gauge, weight, hangers, and QTO. SMACNA HVAC Duct Construction Standards govern gauge-pressure class relationships.
Equipment
AHU · RTU · VAV · FCU · ERV
ARI 430, ARI 340, ARI 880, ARI 440, ARI 1060 equipment family cost and rigging/setting hours per family.
Pipe
CHW · HW · Ref · Gas
ASME B36.10 / B31.9 pipe families, ASHRAE 90.1 insulation thickness, refrigerant ACR copper, gas CS.
Support
Seismic · Hangers · Trapeze
SMACNA seismic restraint and trapeze spacing drive support material, labor, and QTO.
Terminal
Diffusers · Grilles · Linear
SMACNA air distribution devices drive terminal count, ceiling coordination, and QC inspection.
Commercial
QTO → ERP → Cashflow
Standard-driven quantities become CSI Div 23 L/M/C/S/E/R cost-code rows, SOV support, production curves, and executive signals.

Standards-to-controls flow

1. Standards basisSMACNA, ASHRAE, ARI, and NYC Mech Code define families, gauges, insulation thickness, seismic restraint, and equipment selection.
2. GeometryThe model converts selected standards into duct runs, fittings, equipment, and terminal geometry in plan and section.
3. QuantityGeometry drives LF, LB, SF, EA and equipment quantities with formula audit visibility.
4. ERP bridgeQuantities are organized into L/M/C/S/E/R CSI Div 23 cost codes.
5. Field bridgeDaily production and actual cost records update earned value, productivity, and forecast signals.
6. Executive lensCashflow, variance, risk, opportunity, and forecast range move with the same simulation and quantity logic.
